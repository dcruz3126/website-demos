Water heaters don't usually die quietly. Most of the time, they give you a few weeks of warning before they actually quit, you just have to know what to look for. Here's what those warnings usually sound (and look) like.

## 1. The hot water runs out faster than it used to

If a shower that used to last 15 minutes now turns cold in 6, that's often sediment building up at the bottom of the tank, taking up space that used to hold hot water. It's usually fixable with a flush, but it's also one of the earliest signs something's starting to go.

## 2. The water looks a little rusty

A little discoloration right after you turn the tap on and gone within a few seconds is usually just old pipes. Rust that keeps coming, especially from the hot side only, often means the tank itself is corroding from the inside. That one's worth having a plumber look at before it turns into a leak.

## 3. It's making noises it didn't used to make

Popping, rumbling, or a low banging sound usually means sediment has built up on the heating element and is trapping water underneath it, which then boils and pops. Annoying, but not usually an emergency, unless it's paired with any of the other signs on this list.

## 4. There's water pooling around the base

This one's not subtle, and it's not something to wait on. Water at the base of the tank almost always means a crack or a failed valve, and tanks don't heal themselves. If you see this, it's time to call, not wait and check again tomorrow.

## 5. It's past its expected lifespan

Most tank water heaters last somewhere between 8 and 12 years. If yours is creeping past that and showing any of the signs above, it's usually more cost effective to plan a replacement on your schedule than wait for it to fail on its own, usually at the least convenient time possible.

**The good news:** most of these are easy to check in five minutes. If you're not sure which one you're dealing with, that's exactly what we're here for.
